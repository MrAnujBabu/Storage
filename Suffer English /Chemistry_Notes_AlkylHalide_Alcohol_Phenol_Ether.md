# 1️⃣ ALKYL HALIDE (Haloalkanes, R–X)

## 🔹 Nature of C–X bond
- C–X bond **polar** hota hai → Carbon par **δ+** (partial positive), Halogen par **δ–**.
- Isi δ+ carbon par **nucleophile** attack karta hai.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:**
> Halogen carbon se zyada electronegative hai, isliye electron apni taraf kheench leta hai. Carbon "garib" (electron-deficient, +) ho jaata hai. Ab koi bhi electron-rich cheez (nucleophile = "daanveer/donor") us garib carbon ki taraf attract hoti hai. Yahi puri reaction ki jadd (root) hai.

---

## 🔹 Nucleophilic Substitution: SN1 vs SN2 ⭐⭐⭐
> Sir ne kaha: **"Har alternate year NEET mein SN1/SN2 se question aata hai"** — 2025 mein aaya, toh 2026 ReNEET mein expected.

X ko hata kar nucleophile lagane ke **do tareeke** hain:

| | **SN2** | **SN1** |
|---|---|---|
| Tareeka | Jor-jabardasti (nucleophile dhakka maar kar X nikalta hai) | Pyaar se (X khud apne aap nikal jaata hai) |
| Steps | **1 step** (single step) | **2 steps** (X pehle nikalta hai → carbocation banta hai) |
| Rate equation | Rate = k[RX][Nu⁻] | Rate = k[RX] |
| **Order** | **2** | **1** |
| **Molecularity** | **2** | **1** |
| Intermediate | Transition state | **Carbocation** |
| Attack direction | Sirf **back attack** | Front + back dono (planar carbocation) |
| Stereochemistry | **Inversion** (Walden inversion, "chhata ulta") | **Racemisation** (retention + inversion mix) |
| Best substrate (R) | **CH₃ > 1° > 2°** (kam hindrance) | **3° > 2° > 1°** (stable carbocation) |
| Nucleophile chahiye | **Strong** nucleophile | **Weak/normal** nucleophile chalega |
| Leaving group | **Best** chahiye | **Best** chahiye |
| **Solvent** | **Polar aprotic** | **Polar protic** |

> 💡 **Common Sense Box — SN1 vs SN2 ka asli funda:**
> - **SN2 = "ek hi jhatke mein"** — naya banda (nucleophile) purane bande (X) ko peeche se dhakka de kar nikaal deta hai. Isliye **bheed (crowding/hindrance) nahi honi chahiye** — chhota carbon (methyl/1°) best.
> - **SN1 = "pehle khali kursi, phir naya banda"** — X khud uth ke chala jaata hai, kursi (carbocation) khali ho jaati hai, phir koi bhi aa kar baith jaata hai. Yahan kursi **jitni stable** ho utna achha → isliye **3° best** (3° carbocation sabse stable).
> - Yaad rakhne ka shortcut: **"2 = 2 cheezein milti hain (RX + Nu) → order 2 → straightforward 1 step"**, aur **"1 = 1 akela (RX) → order 1 → carbocation"**.

### Reactivity ka golden rule:
> 🔑 **Jin reactions mein carbocation banta hai, unki reactivity ALWAYS carbocation ki stability ke directly proportional hoti hai.**
> Carbocation stability: **3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃** (aur resonance/benzylic/allylic ho toh aur stable).

> ⚠️ **Carbocation rearrangement:** Agar bana hua carbocation kam stable hai aur bagal (adjacent) mein zyada stable banne ka mauka hai (jaise 2° ke bagal mein 3°), toh **hydride/methyl shift** ho kar zyada stable carbocation ban jaata hai. **Question mein ye trap aata hai.**

## 🔹 Leaving Group (LG)
- LG **best** hona chahiye → bond aasani se toote.
- **Down the group leaving tendency INCREASES** → **I⁻ best LG** (Cl⁻ < Br⁻ < I⁻).
- Leaving tendency ∝ **stability of leaving group** (jo anion zyada stable, woh achha LG). Resonance-stabilized anion (jaise CO₃²⁻) achha LG.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** LG woh mehmaan hai jise "jaana" hai. Jo mehmaan aaram se, khushi-khushi nikal jaaye (yaani nikal kar bhi stable rahe), wahi "achha leaving group". Bड़ा aur stable anion (I⁻) charge ko aaram se sambhal leta hai → khushi se nikal jaata hai.

---

## 🔹 Nucleophile (Nu)
- **Nucleophilicity ∝ electron density** (jitni zyada electron density, utna strong nucleophile).
- **Steric hindrance ↑ → nucleophilicity ↓** (bheed se attack nahi ho paata).
- +I groups (jaise CH₃) electron density badhate hain → nucleophilicity badhti hai.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Nucleophile = "electron daan karne wala daanveer". Jiske paas electron zyada (paisa zyada) aur jo patla-dubla ho (kam hindrance, aasani se ghus jaaye) — wahi best nucleophile.

---

## 🔹 Aryl Halide / Vinyl Halide (Benzene/Double bond se直接 attached halogen)
- ⚠️ **Agar halogen DIRECTLY double-bonded carbon ya benzene ring se attached hai → SN1/SN2 NAHI karta (ya slowest karta hai).**
- Wajah: Lone pair resonance → C–X bond mein **partial double bond character** → bond strong → toot-ta nahi.

### Bonus: Benzene derivatives mein SN2 rate
- Yahan **hindrance nahi**, balki **"+" charge** check karte hain. Jitna zyada δ+ carbon par, utni fast reaction.
- **–R groups (multiple bond attached, e.g. –NO₂)** → δ+ badhate hain → reaction fast.
- **+R groups (lone pair attached)** → δ+ ghatate hain → reaction slow.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Vinyl/aryl halide ka halogen "ghar se chipka hua" hai (resonance se bond aadha-double ban gaya). Jo cheez chipki hai woh nikalti nahi → isliye substitution nahi hota.

---

## 🔹 Allylic Bromination — NBS
- **Allylic carbon** = double-bonded carbon ke **adjacent** (bagal wala) carbon.
- **NBS (N-Bromosuccinimide)** = specific reagent jo **allylic position par bromination** karta hai.
- Major product **zyada substituted (3° allylic)** position par.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** "Allylic" sun-te hi dimaag mein laao: **double bond ka padosi carbon**. NBS ek "sniper" hai jo sirf usi padosi (allylic) position ko hit karta hai, double bond ko chhedta nahi.

# 2️⃣ ALCOHOL (R–OH)

## 🔹 Core problem: –OH is a POOR leaving group
- Alcohol direct SN1/SN2 **nahi** karta kyunki **OH⁻ poor LG** hai.
- **Strategy: pehle OH ko good LG mein convert karo, phir substitution.**
- Good LG kaise banayein? → **neutral molecule** (H₂O) bana do (protonate karke) ya complex bana do.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** OH⁻ "zid्दी mehmaan" hai — jaata hi nahi. Toh use pehle **H⁺ khila do (protonate)** → woh **H₂O ban jaata hai → ab khushi se nikal jaata hai** (H₂O neutral, achha LG). Yahi har alcohol reaction ka pehla kadam hai.

### Reactions:
| Reagent | Product | Mechanism note |
|---|---|---|
| **HX (HCl/HBr/HI)** | Alkyl halide (R–X) | Protonate → H₂O nikalta → carbocation → X⁻ attack. **Reactivity ∝ carbocation stability** (3°>2°>1°). HI sabse reactive. **Rearrangement possible.** |
| **PCl₃ / PCl₅ / SOCl₂** | Alkyl chloride | Pehle OH ko good LG banate hain, phir Cl attack. (Sab same type reaction) |
| **Lucas Reagent** = conc. **HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂** | Alkyl chloride (white turbidity) | **Test for 1°/2°/3° alcohol.** 3° → turbidity turant, 2° → 5 min, 1° → no turbidity at room temp. White fume/turbidity = alkyl halide. |

> 💡 **Common Sense Box — Lucas test:** ZnCl₂ ek "helper" (Lewis acid) hai jo OH ko nikalne mein madad karta hai. Carbocation jitna stable (3°), utni jaldi reaction → utni jaldi **dhundla pan (turbidity)** dikhega. So: **jitni jaldi dhundla, utna higher alcohol (3°)**.

---

## 🔹 Oxidation of Alcohols
**Rule:** Oxidation = "OH wale carbon se attached hydrogen ko ek-ek oxygen do." Jab tak us carbon par H hai, oxidation chalta rahega.

| Alcohol | Weak oxidant (PCC, PDC, Cu powder) | Strong oxidant (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇ = "KKJ") |
|---|---|---|
| **1° (Primary)** | → **Aldehyde** (rukta hai) | → **Carboxylic acid (COOH)** |
| **2° (Secondary)** | → **Ketone** | → **Ketone** (always ketone) |
| **3° (Tertiary)** | No oxidation | No oxidation |

- **Ketone ka further oxidation nahi hota** (normally).
- **Jones reagent** = acidic chromic acid (CrO₃/H₂SO₄) in acetone → 1° → COOH.
- **Special:** **Cu powder @ 300°C** par **3° alcohol → Alkene** (dehydration type).

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Oxidation = "carbon se H chheenna aur O jodna".
> - 1° par 2 hydrogen → 2 baar oxidise ho sakta (aldehyde → acid). Weak oxidant beech mein (aldehyde) rok deta; strong oxidant poora kheench leta (acid).
> - 2° par 1 hydrogen → ek hi baar (ketone), phir us carbon par H bacha hi nahi → ruk gaya.
> - 3° par **koi H nahi** OH wale carbon par → oxidation ho hi nahi sakta. Simple!

---

## 🔹 Dehydration of Alcohol
- Conc. acid (H₂SO₄) ke saath alcohol → **Alkene** (–H₂O).
- Reactivity: **3° > 2° > 1°** (carbocation stability).
- **Saytzeff product** (zyada substituted alkene) = major.

# 3️⃣ PHENOL (C₆H₅–OH)

## 🔹 Acidity of Phenol
- Phenol acidic hai (phenoxide ion resonance-stabilized).
- ✅ Phenol **NaOH se react karta hai** (salt + water).
- ❌ Phenol (aur **mono-substituted nitrophenol**) **NaHCO₃ se react NAHI karta** (NaHCO₃ weak base, phenol itna acidic nahi).
- ✅ **Di-/Tri-substituted nitrophenol** (jaise 2,4,6-trinitrophenol = picric acid) → **NaHCO₃ se react karta hai → CO₂ (turbidity/effervescence)**.

> 🔑 **PYQ logic:** Jaise-jaise phenol par **–NO₂ (electron-withdrawing, –R)** groups badhte hain, acidic strength badhti hai → tabhi weak base NaHCO₃ se bhi react kar paata hai.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box — acidity scale:**
> "Acidic banne" ka matlab H⁺ chhodne ke baad bacha anion (phenoxide) stable ho. –NO₂ group us negative charge ko "kha jaata hai" (spread kar deta hai) → anion stable → zyada acidic. Ek NO₂ se kaam nahi banta NaHCO₃ ke liye, par teen NO₂ (picric acid) toh itna acidic ki carbonic acid jaisa weak acid bhi nikal jaata.
> Reference scale yaad rakho: **Carboxylic acid > Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) > Phenol.** Isliye phenol NaHCO₃ ko nahi tod paata.

---

## 🔹 Bromination of Phenol
- **Phenol + Br₂ water (ya Br₂+NaOH/KOH, ya NaOBr)** → **2,4,6-Tribromophenol (white precipitate)**.
- Wajah: Polar solvent mein phenol → **phenoxide ion** (zyada reactive) → saari ortho & para positions fill.
- **Phenol + Br₂ in CS₂/CHCl₃ (non-polar, low temp)** → **mono-bromophenol** (mainly para).
- 🧪 **Test of phenol:** Br₂ (brown) phenol mein daalne par **color ud jaata hai** (decolourise).

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Pani (polar) mein phenol "super-charged" (phenoxide) ban jaata hai → bahut reactive → Br₂ ko jitni jagah mile (3 positions) sab le leta. Non-polar solvent mein phenol "normal" rehta → sirf 1 Br lagta. **Solvent hi decide karta hai mono vs tri.**

---

## 🔹 Named Reactions of Phenol
| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Kolbe–Schmitt** | CO₂ + NaOH | **Salicylic acid** (ortho-COOH; aspirin ka precursor) |
| **Reimer–Tiemann** | CHCl₃ + NaOH | **Salicylaldehyde** (ortho-CHO) |
| **Kolbe (only)** | CCl₄ + NaOH | Salicylic acid type |
| Reduction (Zn dust) | Zn dust | **Benzene** (OH nikal jaata) |
| Oxidation (Na₂Cr₂O₇/Na₂CrO₄) | — | **Benzoquinone** |

- **Phenol ke ortho par:** OH → catechol type, CHO → **salicylaldehyde**, COOH → **salicylic acid**.
- Ortho product major (intramolecular **hydrogen bonding** ki wajah se).

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Electrophile (CO₂ ya :CCl₂) kamzor hai, toh woh phenol ki "sabse reactive jagah" ortho/para par jud-ta hai. Ortho wala major banta hai kyunki H-bonding usse "pakad" kar stable kar deti hai. **Naam yaad karne ka trick: Kol**be = **CO₂** (dono mein "C-O"), **R**eimer = chlo**R**oform (CHCl₃).

---

## 🔹 Cumene Process (Industrial Phenol)
- **Cumene = Isopropylbenzene** (benzene + isopropyl group).
- **Air oxidation of cumene** → cumene hydroperoxide (intermediate) → acid hydrolysis → **Phenol + Acetone**.
- Sirf **acetone** banta hai (koi aur ketone/aldehyde nahi).

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Cumene process ek "2-for-1 offer" hai — ek hi reaction se **phenol AUR acetone** dono mil jaate, isliye industry ko sasta padta. Yaad rakho: **Cumene → Phenol + Acetone**.

# 4️⃣ ETHER (R–O–R)

## 🔹 Williamson Synthesis (Ether banane ka method)
- **Alkyl halide + Sodium alkoxide (R–O⁻Na⁺)** in **dry ether** → **R–O–R (ether)** via **SN2**.
- ⚠️ **Catch:** Alkoxide ek **strong base** bhi hai. Agar alkyl halide **3° (tertiary)** ho → SN2 ki jagah **elimination (alkene)** ho jaata hai.
- ✅ **Rule:** Hamesha **alkyl halide chhota/1° (less hindered)** lo aur **alkoxide bड़े** part se banao.

> 💡 **Common Sense Box:** Alkoxide ke do roop hain — "**nucleophile**" (ether banayega, achha) aur "**strong base**" (alkene banayega, kharab). Agar carbon par bheed (3°) hai, toh base wala roop jeet jaata (elimination). Isliye **substitution chahiye toh halide ko 1°/methyl rakho.**

---

## 🔹 Cleavage of Ether by HI / HBr
- Ether + HX (HI sabse reactive) → ether toot-ta hai.
- **Mechanism rule (sabse important exam point):**
  - **R = 1° ya 2°** → **SN2** → X us carbon par jaata hai jisme **kam carbon (chhota group)** hai; bड़ा part **alcohol** banta hai.
  - **R = 3° / benzylic / resonance-stabilized** → **SN1** → carbocation banta hai jiski stability zyada; X usi par jaata hai.
- Product hamesha: **ek R–X + ek R–OH**.
- **Excess HI** → bacha hua alcohol bhi HI se react kar ke **dono R–I** ban jaate hain (kyunki alcohol + HX bhi reaction deta hai).

> 💡 **Common Sense Box — ether cleavage:**
> Sochо ether do bhai (R–O–R) ko jodne wala "pul (O)" hai. HX aa kar pul tod-ta hai.
> - Agar bhai **chhote/normal (1°,2°)** hain → "**dhakke wali (SN2)**" → I chhote bhai ko milta (kam bheed), bड़े bhai ko OH.
> - Agar koi bhai **3°/benzylic (VIP)** hai → woh khud stable carbocation ban kar nikal jaata (SN1) → I usi VIP ko milta.
> Aur agar **HI extra** hai toh bachi-khuchi alcohol bhi I mein badal jaati → dono I.

# 📝 IMPORTANT QUESTIONS & PYQs (Lecture mein karaye gaye)

> Sir ne ye actual exam-pattern questions aur PYQs discuss kiye. Inhe zaroor practice karein.

### ⭐ PYQ-1 (NEET 2025) — *Har alternate year SN1/SN2 PYQ*
**Q.** Which one is **most reactive towards SN1 reaction**?
(Compounds: 2 benzene rings + methyl / 1 benzene + methyl / etc.)
**✅ Answer logic:** Jiska **carbocation sabse stable** (yahan **2 benzene ring wala = 3°/dibenzylic**) → wahi most reactive.
**Trick:** SN1 reactivity ∝ carbocation stability.

### ⭐ PYQ-2 (NEET 2025) — Nitrile/reaction
**Q.** Reaction involving attack on **CN** → product converts to ketone, and another to ammonia (excess condition).
**✅ Key:** Excess reagent par dhyaan do — product change ho jaata hai.

### ⭐ PYQ-3 (NEET 2023) — Ether cleavage
**Q.** Consider the following reaction (ether + HX). Identify product.
**✅ Answer logic:** 1°/2° → SN2 (I chhote carbon par); excess HI → dono iodide.

### ⭐ PYQ-4 (NEET 2014) — Phenol acidity
**Q.** Which molecule reacts with **NaHCO₃** to give effervescence (CO₂ / turbidity)?
**✅ Answer:** **Di-/tri-substituted nitrophenol** (e.g., picric acid). Plain phenol & mono-nitrophenol → NO reaction.

### ⭐ PYQ-5 — Cumene process intermediate
**Q.** Structure of **intermediate 'A'** in air oxidation of cumene?
**✅ Answer:** **Cumene hydroperoxide** → hydrolysis → Phenol + Acetone.

### ⭐ Practice Questions (concept-based, lecture mein solve kiye):
1. **Which is most reactive towards HBr?** → Jiska carbocation most stable (3°). *Check for rearrangement!*
   - e.g., 2-methylprop-2-ol (3°) > others.
2. **NBS reaction — major product?** → Allylic bromination at **more substituted (3°) allylic** position.
3. **Correct order of reactivity for SN2** (given compounds) → **Least hindered / smallest alkyl group fastest** (CH₃ > 1° > 2° > 3°).
4. **Lucas test** — identify 1°/2°/3° alcohol by **rate of turbidity**.
5. **Williamson synthesis** — which alkyl halide to choose? → **Less hindered (1°)** to avoid elimination.
6. **Phenol + excess Br₂ water** → **2,4,6-tribromophenol** (white ppt).
7. **R/S configuration in SN2** → **inversion** (configuration change); **SN1** → racemisation/no retention.

# 🎯 EXAM REVISION — One-Line Power Points

1. C–X polar → carbon δ+ → nucleophile attacks.
2. **SN2** = 1 step, order 2, inversion, polar aprotic, **CH₃>1°>2°**, strong Nu.
3. **SN1** = 2 steps, order 1, racemisation, polar protic, **3°>2°>1°**, carbocation.
4. Reactivity (carbocation reactions) ∝ **carbocation stability** (always!).
5. Best leaving group **down the group**: I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻.
6. **Vinyl/Aryl halide** → no SN1/SN2 (partial double bond).
7. **NBS** → allylic bromination.
8. Alcohol: OH = poor LG → **protonate → H₂O (good LG)** → reacts.
9. **Lucas reagent** = HCl + anhyd. ZnCl₂ → test 1°/2°/3° (turbidity speed).
10. Oxidation: 1°→aldehyde(weak)/acid(strong); 2°→ketone; 3°→no oxidation.
11. Phenol: NaOH ✓, NaHCO₃ ✗ (but di/tri-nitrophenol ✓).
12. Phenol + Br₂ water → **2,4,6-tribromophenol** (white ppt); test = decolourisation.
13. **Kolbe** → salicylic acid (CO₂); **Reimer-Tiemann** → salicylaldehyde (CHCl₃).
14. **Cumene** → Phenol + Acetone.
15. **Williamson** → ether via SN2; avoid 3° (elimination).
16. **Ether + HI:** 1°/2°→SN2 (I to smaller C); 3°/benzylic→SN1; excess HI→both iodides.
